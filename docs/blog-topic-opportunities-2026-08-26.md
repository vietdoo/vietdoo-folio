# Blog topic audit and opportunity shortlist — 2026-08-26

## Audit conclusion

The current folio has **110 Markdown files grouped into 57 translation keys**. Its strongest editorial pattern is production AI engineering: agent reliability, evaluation, observability, MCP and tool security, RAG and memory, routing and failover, cost, identity, approvals, sandboxing, streaming, deployment, and release operations. The live index confirms that the recent sequence already includes context engineering, prompt-injection boundaries, observability without data leaks, multimodal RAG, deletion guarantees, decision traces, telemetry, and provider rotation.

The opportunity is therefore not another generic “how to build an AI agent” post. The best next article should focus on a **missing control boundary**: a concrete failure mode, an inspectable contract, and an operational rollout. Anthropic frames context engineering as curating the tokens available during inference, while NIST frames AI RMF as a way to incorporate trustworthiness into AI design, development, use, and evaluation.[1] [2] A separate 2026 research direction describes firewall-like projections that limit what information crosses agent boundaries.[3] These sources support the direction, but the proposed article will remain an original production pattern rather than presenting “context firewall” as a formal standard.

## Selected topic

The strongest addition is **The Context Firewall: Governing What Enters the Model**. It is the clearest gap after screening both slugs and article bodies. The folio already explains how to optimize long-running context, prevent prompt-injection confusion, redact observability data, and delete durable agent memory. It does not yet explain the decision that happens **before inference**: which fields may cross into the model, for which purpose, under which tenant and authority scope, in what transformed representation, and with what evidence that the decision was enforced.

The selected article will use the shared translation key `context-firewall-pre-inference-data-governance`, category `security`, `draft: false`, and the randomly selected publication date **2026-04-12**. The hero will also serve as the thumbnail. Three additional in-article visuals will explain the admission flow, context envelope, and deny/audit path. The article will distinguish an application-level design pattern from a network firewall and from legal advice.
