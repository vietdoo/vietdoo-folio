## Why these recommendations fit the folio

The strongest editorial opportunity is not another introductory “what is an AI agent?” article. The folio already speaks to readers who care about production boundaries, failure modes and architectural trade-offs. The next series should therefore convert current standards and recurring production problems into implementation playbooks with diagrams, pseudo-code, test cases and decision tables.

OpenTelemetry’s dedicated GenAI semantic-conventions repository covers spans, metrics and events for GenAI clients, MCP and provider-specific integrations, making interoperability a credible standards-led subject rather than a speculative trend [1]. The MCP specification defines lifecycle, authorization, resources, prompts, tools, roots, sampling and elicitation as distinct protocol areas, leaving room for a lifecycle/compatibility article that does not rehash security [2]. OpenAI’s eval-driven system-design cookbook explicitly connects small labeled seeds, initial evals, business KPIs, cost impact and iterative deployment, which supports the first recommendation [3]. OWASP’s 2025 sensitive-information-disclosure guidance supports a context-firewall article centered on sanitization, least privilege, tokenization and redaction [4]. Time-aware RAG is also an active research direction, with recent work framing temporal retrieval and diachronic question answering as distinct problems from ordinary semantic retrieval [5].

## Selection decision

The three selected posts are **Eval-Driven AI Systems**, **Temporal RAG**, and **GenAI Telemetry That Travels**. Together they create a coherent mini-series: first define how to know an AI system is improving, then handle historical truth in retrieval, then instrument the whole system through a portable telemetry contract. They also avoid the folio’s already-covered clusters while reinforcing its senior-production identity.
